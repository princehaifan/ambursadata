# Findings
Pipeline step: 3 of 6 — ambursaData
Date: 16 November 2026
Business question: How are people in Exeter using AI, and where does Exeter differ from the UK picture?

---

## Sanity checks run before drawing conclusions
- **Exeter adoption estimate cross-checked two independent ways.** Method A (Q7, age-standardisation) gives 66–67%. Method B (student composition) assumes Exeter's students (15.8% of residents aged 16+) use AI at HEPI's 95% and everyone else uses it at the UK non-student rate implied by DSIT (56.9%). That gives **62.9%**. Both methods land above the UK's 59%, so we report a **range of 63–67%**, not a single figure.
- **Headcount check:** 138,399 residents (mid-2024) × 85.2% aged 16+ (Census 2021) ≈ **117,900 adults**. A rate of 63–67% means roughly 74,000–79,000 adult users and 39,000–44,000 non-users.
- **Business figures:** Lloyds says 66% of South West firms use AI, while ONS says 35% of UK firms with 10+ employees do. That gap isn't believable as a real difference, so it's treated as a definitional anomaly (see Anomalies), not a finding.

---

## Top 3 Findings

### Finding 1: Exeter is probably one of the UK's heavier AI-using places, but its non-users are concentrated among older residents
**In plain English:** Exeter's population is unusually young and full of students, and age is the strongest predictor of AI use. So an estimated two in three Exeter adults have used generative AI, against about six in ten nationally. But that leaves roughly 40,000 adults who haven't, and around half of them are aged 65 or over.
**The numbers:**
- Estimated Exeter generative AI use: **63–67%**, against **59%** for the UK (DSIT 2025/26). Two methods were used: an age index of 1.12–1.14 against England, and a student-composition estimate.
- 40.3% of Exeter adults are aged 16–34, against 29.8% in England and 26.9% in the South West. 15.8% of residents aged 16+ are full-time students, against 5.6% in England.
- National non-use by age: 37% (18–29), 64% (30–49), 78% (50–64), **89% (65+)**. These come from University of Exeter/YouGov, March 2025.
- Exeter has about 23,100 adults aged 65+. At 2025 national rates, about **20,600** of them weren't using generative AI.
**Why it matters:** "Exeter is AI-savvy" and "Exeter has a large AI-excluded group" are both true. Support aimed at "the public" in general would mostly reach people who already use AI.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM. Two methods agree on the direction and roughly on size, but it's modelled, not measured.
**Limitation:** There's no Exeter resident survey, and the DSIT South West row is missing. The age gradient is from March 2025, and older people's use has probably risen since, so 20,600 is an upper estimate.

### Finding 2: Exeter students use AI as a tutor, not a ghostwriter, and usage is running ahead of trust and support
**In plain English:** Almost every Exeter student uses AI, and mostly to understand their subject, not to write their work for them. But four in five have caught it getting things wrong, more than a third often worry about being wrongly accused of AI misuse, and the most-wanted fix is hands-on training.
**The numbers:**
- 92% have used AI for academic work (only 8% never have). ChatGPT reaches **84%**, with Gemini at 39% and Copilot at 35%. So the University's licensed tool (Copilot) reaches well under half as many students as ChatGPT.
- Understanding a concept: **71%**, against 61% of UK undergraduates explaining concepts for assessed work (+10 points). Summarising readings: 43% against 49% (−6). Research help: 45% against 40% (+5).
- **79%** have noticed AI giving false information, and **37%** worry fairly or very often about being flagged or penalised.
- Support wanted: **workshops or training 55%**, clearer faculty guidelines 19%. Only 10% want AI banned.
- Even in the best-served faculty (HLS), only **50%** received helpful guidance. 60% say AI skills are an important part of their education.
**Why it matters:** The demand is for capability (using AI well and checking it), not restriction. The University's approved tools have weaker reach than the free consumer tool students actually use.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM. The Guild panel is self-selecting and doesn't publish its respondent count. The direction is consistent with HEPI's national data (95% use; 49% say AI improved their experience).
**Limitation:** Cross-survey comparisons use differently worded questions, so the +10 and −6 gaps are directional only.

### Finding 3: Exeter's AI refusers object on values, not on lack of know-how
**In plain English:** Exeter students who avoid AI mostly do so because of its environmental impact or because they want to think for themselves. That's quite different from the UK pattern, where non-users mainly say they'd rather not, or worry about privacy and not knowing enough.
**The numbers:**
- Exeter student non-users: **environmental impact 58%**, self-reliance 20%, ethical concerns 11%.
- UK adult non-users: prefer not to 44%, privacy or security 33%, don't know enough 32%, accuracy worries 31%.
- In HASS, **75%** agree AI risks replacing critical thinking (ESE 60%, HLS 58%). 10% of students want education on AI's environmental impact.
- In 2025, 68% of Exeter students were concerned about AI and data privacy.
**Why it matters:** A "here's how to use it" message won't move this group. Engagement that takes the environmental cost and the protection of critical thinking seriously will.
**Confidence:** LOW–MEDIUM. The non-user base is a small, unpublished subset of the panel, and the two surveys offered different answer lists.
**Limitation:** "Environmental impact" wasn't offered as an option in DSIT, so we can't say UK non-users don't hold that view.

## Supporting Findings
- **Personal and wellbeing use is real.** 18% of Exeter students have used AI for advice on a personal issue and 18% to understand medical symptoms, while 4% used it to feel less alone and 4% for help with depression. For comparison, 10% of UK adult users have used it for emotional or relationship issues. The questions differ, but the scale suggests the University's wellbeing services should know this is happening.
- **Exeter's big institutions are already deploying AI in front-line work.** Royal Devon has used an AI scribe in 2,700+ outpatient consultations and projects up to ~15,000 extra appointments a year at full rollout. It's also trialling MEMORI infection detection and SAMueL-2 stroke ML. The Met Office is building FastNet. Devon County Council and East Devon use Copilot (East Devon used it to summarise 1.1m+ words of planning feedback). The University runs an approved-tools catalogue, and Age UK Exeter has an AI policy. Health is the most active sector in the register.
- **The public is cautious about AI in high-stakes outputs.** Nationally, only ~45% rate AI weather forecasts as accurate, against ~78% for physics-based ones, which matters for the Exeter-headquartered Met Office. 74% of UK adults worry AI information may be inaccurate.
- **South West businesses are adopting fast, but small firms lag.** Lloyds: 50% (June 2025) → 66% (March 2026) of South West firms use AI. OpenAI's SME survey: 28% of South West SMEs don't use AI at all, against 19% UK-wide and only 7% non-use in London.

## Anomalies
1. **Business figures that can't both be right (definitional).** South West 66% (Lloyds, Mar 2026) against UK 35% (ONS, June 2026, 10+ employees). Possible explanations: Lloyds counts any AI use, including free tools, while ONS asks about specific AI technologies; the panel compositions differ; and Lloyds reaches more engaged firms. Treat this as a measurement gap, not a regional lead.
2. **"Self-taught" students (positive and surprising).** 60% of Exeter students say they had no prior AI experience in a learning setting (67% of UK-domiciled students), yet 84% use ChatGPT. So most students learned AI on their own, outside teaching. That's consistent with the guidance gap in Finding 2.
3. **Missing data (MISSING).** There's no representative Exeter resident survey, the DSIT South West regional row (table E6) is missing, and there are no Exeter-specific business figures. These are the largest gaps in the evidence base.
4. **A policy turnaround at Devon County Council.** An earlier FOI response stated that the council "has not approved the use of generative AI software"; by April 2026 it had a published Copilot policy. This is a fast institutional shift, noted from the initiative sources.

## Facts vs. Interpretations vs. Hypotheses
CONFIRMED FACTS (data shows directly):
  • 59% of UK adults used generative AI in the last three months (DSIT 2025/26); usage falls steeply with age.
  • 40.3% of Exeter adults are aged 16–34 and 15.8% of residents aged 16+ are students (Census 2021).
  • Among Exeter students: 84% have used ChatGPT, 71% use AI to understand concepts, 79% have seen false info, 37% worry often about being flagged, and 55% want workshops.
  • Exeter student non-users most often cite environmental impact (58%).
  • Royal Devon, the Met Office, Devon County Council, East Devon, the University and Age UK Exeter all have documented AI deployments or policies.

INTERPRETATIONS (what we believe this means):
  • Exeter's overall AI use is above the UK average (63–67%) because of its age and student profile.
  • Exeter students treat AI mainly as a learning aid, and support hasn't kept pace with use.
  • Non-use among Exeter students is driven by values more than by skills.

HYPOTHESES (possible explanations, not yet confirmed):
  • Environmental concern among Exeter students reflects the University's sustainability identity (e.g. its Global Systems Institute and climate research).
  • The real Exeter adoption rate is above 67%, because graduates (who use AI more) are over-represented, which the age-only model doesn't capture.
  • Consumer tools dominate because they're easier to use or were adopted before the University's licensed tools became available.

QUESTIONS RAISED (things we should investigate further):
  • What does the DSIT South West row (table E6) show? Does it support the 63–67% estimate?
  • How do Exeter's non-student, non-graduate and older residents actually use AI? This needs a local resident survey (e.g. adding AI questions to Exeter City Council's annual LGA residents survey of 1,100 people).
  • How many Exeter businesses use AI, and for what?
  • What is the Guild panel's respondent count and demographic weighting?
